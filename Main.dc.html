01

INTRODUCTION

  • Brain aneurysm is an abnormal bulging or weakening of a blood vessel in the brain.
  • It may remain unruptured or rupture and cause brain bleeding.
  • Subarachnoid hemorrhage is a major complication of aneurysm rupture.
  • Common types include saccular, fusiform, and dissecting aneurysms.
  • Early detection is important to reduce the risk of rupture and severe neurological complications.
  • Medical imaging such as CT, MRI, and angiography plays an important role in detection.
02

RESEARCH GAP

A largely unexplored approach is using pre-trained medical foundation models as feature extractors and then applying those features to aneurysm detection/classification.

Key gap: No reviewed study has used a foundation model trained on medical CT images to extract transferable features for a downstream brain aneurysm detection/classification task.

03

PROBLEM & OBJECTIVE

Problem statement

Developing an automated solution to accurately and efficiently detect the presence and location of intracranial aneurysms across 13 anatomical locations using brain CT imaging.

Objective
  • Explore using pre-trained AI models to find brain aneurysms across 13 locations.
  • Use the AI model to read brain scan images and pull out important visual clues.
  • Create a tool that uses these clues to correctly spot and label brain aneurysms.
04

DATASET AND EDA

Several thousand brain imaging series from multiple modalities, including CTA, MRA, and MRI. Each series is labeled for aneurysm presence across 13 anatomical vessel locations, along with aneurysm localization coordinates for affected cases.

EDA focuses on
  • Distribution of imaging modalities
  • Aneurysm vs. non-aneurysm class balance
  • Frequency across the 13 anatomical locations
  • Distribution of localized aneurysms and available segmentations

IMPLEMENTATION

1
Prepare the Data
  • Read each CT scan, sort the slices, and apply the vessel brightness window
  • Resample to a uniform scale and cut out the Circle of Willis region
  • Create three projection views (top, front, side) and stack them as one 3-channel image
  • Save the result to disk once so training loads it quickly
2
Build the Model
  • Load a pretrained EfficientNet-B4 as the shared feature extractor
  • Tap it at two points: the Where Map (middle layer) and the Whole-Scan Summary (end of the network)
  • Attach three heads: Global Diagnosis, Artery Identifier, Heatmap Locator
3
Train the Model
  • Combine three losses into one: global, artery, and heatmap
  • Use the focal loss to focus on hard cases and rare arteries
  • Phase 1: warm up with gentle heatmap guidance
  • Phase 2: add model-suggested labels and normal-scan lessons
4
Apply the Common-Sense Check
  • Multiply each artery probability and each heatmap by the overall aneurysm probability
  • Pick the brightest heatmap spot as the marker for the doctor
5
Testing
  • Choose decision thresholds on validation data only
  • Freeze them, then run the unseen test data once
